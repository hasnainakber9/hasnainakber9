## Title
Confident Where Nobody Looked: Testing AI Before It Fills Gaps in Displacement Data

## Abstract (197 words)
Migration actors are starting to use AI chatbots to fill gaps in local data. In displacement settings these gaps are widest, and women are among the least counted. This article draws on two studies to show why AI estimates must be tested before they inform migration policy and programming. In the first, five open-weight AI models answered 1,200 questions about women's lives (child marriage, home births, anaemia, partner violence and menstrual hygiene), drawn from 4,253 subnational figures in Demographic and Health Surveys across 61 countries. The models were overconfident: their 80% ranges contained the true figure only 16% to 51% of the time, and none beat simply quoting the national figure. Where little research existed, answers barely reflected local differences, and telling a model how much evidence existed helped no more than a false count. The second study analyses IOM DTM flood-response assessments of 12,820 villages in Pakistan. Their questionnaires, of 327 and 286 fields answered by village key informants, contain no question on child marriage or disability, and only a dignity-kit item on menstruation. The article proposes a low-cost protocol for testing AI estimates against DTM and survey data, and practical steps to close these gaps.
