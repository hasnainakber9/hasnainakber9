## Workflow corner
- **Local-day rainfall totals from hourly ERA5-Land, done right for Pakistan (UTC+5):** shift the time stamps by five hours before resampling, so that each "day" runs from midnight to midnight Pakistan time. ERA5-Land's hourly precipitation is a running total that restarts each day, so turn it into hourly amounts first: difference consecutive hours, except at 01 UTC, where the stored value already is the first hour. In xarray: `d = ds.tp.diff("time")`, then `tp_h = d.where(d.time.dt.hour != 1, ds.tp.sel(time=d.time))`, then `tp_h.assign_coords(time=tp_h.time + pd.Timedelta("5h")).resample(time="1D").sum()`. ARCO-ERA5 (plain ERA5) already stores hourly amounts, so there you only shift and resample.

## Methods note
**Random cross-validation flatters a model that you will use somewhere new.** The TRACE paper (item @@TRACE@@) corrected ERA5-Land temperatures with machine learning. The error fell by 47% when the test days were picked at random, but only by 26% when whole stations were held out, and by 23% when the model trained in the lowlands was used in the mountains. Its edge over a simple elevation correction shrank from 22 points to 2. Random splits leak information because neighbouring days and stations look alike.

The same trap appears in panel econometrics: a model fitted on Punjab districts and judged on more Punjab districts says little about Gilgit-Baltistan. Two habits fix most of it:
- **hold out whole units:** leave out entire districts or regions when you test, not random rows;
- **report the simple baseline:** show elevation-only or climatology alongside the fancy model, so readers can see what the extra machinery buys.

The Wular Lake deposit (item @@WULAR@@) shows a cousin of this problem: a trend that was really a change in how often the satellite looked. When a trend correlates 0.87 with the number of observations, normalise by observation count before you believe it.
