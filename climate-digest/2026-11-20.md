## Shortlist

### 1. Bridging the end of MODIS: harmonized land-surface anomalies for the 2026–27 El Niño (dataset + code)
Ahmed et al. (University of Dhaka, BRAC University) · Zenodo · 18 Nov 2026 · https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24354955 · open (CC BY 4.0)
- **What's new:** monthly and 3-month anomaly maps up to October 2026, on a 0.25° global grid, set against 2015–16 and 2023–24.
  - Layers: land temperature, EVI2 greenness, CHIRPS v3 rain, SMAP soil moisture and burned area.
  - MODIS data from 2003–2021 is mapped onto the scale of VIIRS, its successor sensor.
  - The z-scores leave out the event's own years.
- **Why it matters:** the fastest check of whether this winter over the Indus plains and the HKH looks like 2015–16.
- **Reproducible?** ✅ About 4 GB of NetCDF files need no login; code is at GitHub rezw4n/ensosat. Version 0.0 is labelled preliminary, and I found no validation numbers yet.
- **Verdict:** FULL READ

### 2. Drying winters, greening valleys: drivers of vegetation change across Afghanistan's drylands, 2003–2024 (dataset + code)
Amiri, Conteh, JunYuan · Zenodo · posted 15 Nov 2026 · https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24099957 · open (CC BY 4.0)
- **What's new:** a panel of 394 districts over 22 years, built from CHIRPS and MODIS NDVI. The record has no abstract, so these figures come from its results files.
  - Winter rain fell by 2.1 mm a year (p = 0.0002), yet 194 districts greened.
  - A residual-trend method calls 180 districts' greening "non-climatic", for example new irrigation.
  - A two-way fixed-effects model gives a greenness-to-rain elasticity of 0.22 (SE 0.039).
- **Why it matters:** your methods applied to satellite data across the border from Chitral, and the template for this week's research target.
- **Reproducible?** ✅ Python code is included (folder paths are hard-coded). There is no paper, no peer review and no affiliation listed, so cite it with care.
- **Verdict:** FULL READ (code)

### 3. TRACE: transferring a machine-learning bias correction of ERA5-Land from lowlands to high mountains (paper)
E. Gül · *Journal of Hydrometeorology* · 18 Nov 2026 · https://doi.org/10.1175/jhm-d-26-0122.1 · access not verified (the AMS page blocked my reader)
- **What's new:** a boosted-tree correction of ERA5-Land at 67 stations in Türkiye, tested on harder and harder splits.
  - Temperature error falls 47% on random days, but only 23% when the model is trained in the lowlands and used in the mountains.
  - Its edge over an elevation-only correction shrinks from 22 points to 2.
  - Its 90% intervals cover only 62% of cases after transfer.
- **Why it matters:** Gilgit-Baltistan is the same setting, with stations in the valleys and few above them.
- **Reproducible?** 🟡 ERA5-Land is public; I couldn't see the station source or the code.
- **Verdict:** FULL READ

### 4. GPROF V08: improving satellite precipitation estimates across passive-microwave sensors (preprint)
Pfreundschuh, Brown, Kummerow · arXiv:2611.19978 · 15 Nov 2026 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19978 · open
- **What's new:** GPROF V08, the rain retrieval that feeds IMERG, swaps its Bayesian lookup for a neural network. Correlation improves by up to 40% for two sensor types over ocean. Biases are "of the same order" as in V07.
- **Why it matters:** this is the input behind IMERG V08 (see alerts). There are no stated gains over land or mountains.
- **Reproducible?** 🟡 No code is listed.
- **Verdict:** QUICK SCAN

### 5. Political connections and drought-induced migration among farm households in India (paper)
Sreekumar et al. · *Journal of Development Studies* · 18 Nov 2026 · https://doi.org/10.1080/00220388.2026.2744431 · paywalled
- **What's new:** links IMD-based drought to the two-round India Human Development Survey household panel.
  - Drought raises the number of migrants from farm households.
  - The effect is smaller where households have political connections, which the authors trace to better access to crop insurance and the rural job guarantee.
- **Why it matters:** the design ports to Pakistan almost unchanged: CHIRPS rainfall plus PSLM survey rounds, with BISP access as the moderator.
- **Reproducible?** 🟡 The IHDS data are public; no code. The abstract gives no effect sizes.
- **Verdict:** QUICK SCAN

### 6. Crop-specific impacts of the 2023/24 drought on Zambia's agricultural production (paper, late pick)
Mulungu et al. · *Climate Services* · 25 Aug 2026 · https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cliser.2026.100710 · open access
- **What's new:** a district fixed-effects model of official yields over 8 seasons, against a CHIRPS rainfall index (SPI).
  - A one-standard-deviation rain deficit cuts yields by 30% for maize and 12% for millet.
  - 76% of the 2023/24 loss in crop value is attributed to the El Niño drought.
- **Why it matters:** a clean, open yield template for our thinnest topic, ENSO → crops. ENSO enters only as a single event, not as an index.
- **Reproducible?** 🟡 CHIRPS is public; yields and code are available on request.
- **Verdict:** QUICK SCAN

### 7. Characterizing ENSO teleconnections' impact on gross primary productivity across CMIP6 models (paper)
Salazar, Keppel-Aleks, Steiner · *Journal of Climate* · 16 Nov 2026 · https://doi.org/10.1175/JCLI-D-26-0172.1 · paywalled
- **What's new:** across 10 climate models, land carbon uptake falls by 0.68 ± 0.18 billion tonnes of carbon per °C of Niño-3.4, mostly through lower photosynthesis. The effect is clearest in the Amazon, Southeast Asia and Australia.
- **Why it matters:** a peer-reviewed ENSO → vegetation result. It is model-only, and South Asia isn't a hotspot, which fits the idea that El Niño reaches Pakistan's winter through rain rather than heat.
- **Reproducible?** 🟡 CMIP6 data are public; there is no code statement.
- **Verdict:** QUICK SCAN

### 8. Water permanence rather than extent at a Himalayan Ramsar wetland: Wular Lake, 1984–2025 (dataset + code)
Farooq, De, Zia · Zenodo · 15 Nov 2026 · https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24247498 · open
- **What's new:** Landsat sees the lake far more often now (a median of 12 usable months a year, up from 7). An apparent rise in long-lasting water correlates 0.87 with the number of observations, and disappears once water is measured as a share of the observed months.
- **Why it matters:** the Jhelum is part of the Indus system, and this is a clear warning for any long satellite trend in the HKH.
- **Reproducible?** ✅ The Earth Engine script and Python code are included; the paper is under review.
- **Verdict:** QUICK SCAN

## Workflow corner
- **Local-day rain totals from hourly ERA5-Land for Pakistan:** the hourly value is a running total that restarts each day, so convert it to hourly amounts first, then shift the clock by five hours.
  - `d = ds.tp.diff("time")`
  - `tp_h = d.where(d.time.dt.hour != 1, ds.tp.sel(time=d.time))`
  - `tp_h.assign_coords(time=tp_h.time + pd.Timedelta("5h")).resample(time="1D").sum()`
  - ARCO-ERA5 already stores hourly amounts, so there you only shift and resample.
- **End-of-MODIS record (item 1):** open one file of 3-month z-scores and plot an Indus box. That gives a one-figure check of this winter against 2015–16.

## Methods note
**Random cross-validation flatters a model you will use somewhere new.** In TRACE (item 3), the skill measured with random test days roughly halved when the model was moved to unseen mountains. Neighbouring days and stations resemble each other, so random splits leak information.

The same trap applies to district panels: a model judged on Punjab says little about Gilgit-Baltistan. Two habits help:
- hold out whole provinces when you test;
- always report the simple baseline beside the clever model.

The Wular Lake deposit (item 8) shows a related trap. If a trend correlates with the number of satellite observations, normalise by that count before believing it.

## Next research target
**Do wetter winters green Pakistan's rain-fed districts in spring, and more so in El Niño winters?** This replaces last week's wheat yields, which are patchy by district, with satellite greenness, which exists for every district and year. It copies the Afghanistan design (item 2).
- **Data:** CHIRPS Oct–Mar rain and MODIS NDVI for Feb–Apr by district, through Earth Engine; DJF ONI from NOAA CPC; a rain-fed flag from GFSAD cropland or the Indus crop-water rasters.
- **Method:** NDVI(d,t) = β·rain_z(d,t) + γ·rain_z(d,t)×ONI_t + α_d + δ_t + ε. Use Driscoll-Kraay standard errors, which allow districts' errors to be correlated within a year: `xtscc` in Stata or `vcov = "DK"` in R `fixest`. Then split rain-fed from irrigated districts.
- **First 3 steps:**
  1. Run the starter for 2003–2025.
  2. Add the rain-fed flag and map β by province.
  3. Test by holding out whole provinces (see Methods note).
- **Output:** a short note and an AOGS2027 abstract (due 15 Jan). With quick work it could also add a district result to the EGU abstract due 1 Dec.
- **Risks:**
  - NDVI saturates over dense irrigated crops;
  - February snow depresses NDVI in the north;
  - 23 winters contain only a handful of El Niño years.

**Starter code** (`climate-digest/starter/2026-11-20_pakistan_winter_rain_spring_green_panel.py`). It compiles, and I ran it for two winters: 126 districts each, with mean winter rain of 150 mm in 2016 and 132 mm in 2024.
```python
import io, os, sys, requests, pandas as pd
sys.path.insert(0, "climate-digest/tools"); import ee_auth
ee = ee_auth.init()
dist = ee.FeatureCollection("WM/geoLab/geoBoundaries/600/ADM2").filter(ee.Filter.eq("shapeGroup", "PAK"))
chirps = ee.ImageCollection("UCSB-CHG/CHIRPS/PENTAD").select("precipitation")
ndvi = ee.ImageCollection("MODIS/061/MOD13A2").select("NDVI")          # 16-day, 1 km, scale 0.0001
years = [int(y) for y in os.environ.get("YEARS", ",".join(map(str, range(2003, 2026)))).split(",")]
bands = []
for y in years:                                                     # winter ending in year y: Oct (y-1) to Mar (y)
    rain = chirps.filterDate(f"{y-1}-10-01", f"{y}-04-01").sum().rename(f"rain_{y}")
    green = ndvi.filterDate(f"{y}-02-01", f"{y}-05-01").mean().multiply(0.0001).rename(f"ndvi_{y}")
    bands += [rain, green]
stats = ee.Image.cat(bands).reduceRegions(dist, ee.Reducer.mean(), scale=5000, tileScale=4).getInfo()["features"]
rows = [{"district": f["properties"]["shapeName"], "id": f["properties"]["shapeID"], "year": y,
         "winter_rain_mm": f["properties"].get(f"rain_{y}"), "spring_ndvi": f["properties"].get(f"ndvi_{y}")}
        for f in stats for y in years]
df = pd.DataFrame(rows).dropna()
g = df.groupby("id").winter_rain_mm
df["rain_z"] = (df.winter_rain_mm - g.transform("mean")) / g.transform("std")      # standardised within district
oni = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(requests.get("https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/data/indices/oni.ascii.txt", timeout=60).text), sep=r"\s+")
oni.columns = ["seas", "year", "total", "oni"]
djf = oni[oni.seas == "DJF"][["year", "oni"]].rename(columns={"oni": "oni_djf"})   # DJF of year y = the winter ending in y
df = df.merge(djf, on="year", how="left")
df.to_csv("pak_winter_rain_spring_ndvi_panel.csv", index=False)
print(df.groupby("year")[["winter_rain_mm", "spring_ndvi", "oni_djf"]].mean().round(3))
```
