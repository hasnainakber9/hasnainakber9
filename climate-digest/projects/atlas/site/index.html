    Method

    How the numbers are made, and their limits

    Population at 1 km, multiplied through each layer and summed over province and district outlines. Every layer has gaps; they are stated here and beside the map.

    All method notes 17

    Women and girls

    LandScan Global 2024 population at 1 km, multiplied by the local share of women aged 15–49 and girls aged 10–14 from GPWv4.11 (2010 census round). Age shares shift slowly, but they are a decade and a half old.

    Freezing nights

    the share of MODIS Aqua 8-day night land-surface-temperature composites below 0 °C, 2016–2025, times 365. The satellite sees the ground only on clear nights, which run colder than cloudy ones.

    Extreme heat

    days a year with a maximum air temperature of 35 °C or more, 2021–2025, from ERA5-Land at 9 km. It misses heat in narrow valleys and ignores humidity.

    Flooded land

    the Global Flood Database maps 913 large floods seen by the MODIS satellites from 2000 to 2018, at 250 m. Land counts as flooded if it stayed under water for three days or more in at least one of them and lies within 10 m of height above the nearest river or the sea; that second test removes mountain and building shadows that the satellite mistakes for water. Each 1 km cell gets the share of its area that flooded, and women and girls are counted in proportion. The record stops in 2018, so it misses Pakistan’s 2022 floods; it also misses floods hidden by cloud, flash floods shorter than three days and most flooding inside cities. Some false alarms survive the filter, mostly tidal flats and persistent cloud shadow near coasts (Scotland’s 10% is too high).

    Far from care

    walking time to the nearest health facility, Malaria Atlas Project 2019. The facility map is incomplete in many countries, so distances can be overstated.

    Exposed and far

    two hours’ walk or more from care, and living with 30 or more freezing nights a year, 30 or more hot days a year, or on land that has flooded.

    Flood of 2022, Pakistan

    Sentinel-1 radar. A 100 m pixel counts as flooded if it was open water between 20 August and 15 September 2022 and not in the same weeks of 2021, is not permanent or seasonal water, and lies on flat, low ground. Radar misses water under crops, trees and between buildings, and water that came and went between satellite passes, so this is a low estimate of where water stood, not of who was affected.

    Failed rains

    CHIRPS rainfall, 1981–2025, between 50°S and 50°N. A rain year runs July to June. A year counts as failed if it brought less than three quarters of the 1981–2010 average. Women and girls are counted where two or more of the last ten rain years failed, and only in rain-fed farming areas: 10% or more cropland nearby (Copernicus land cover 2019), less than half of it irrigated, and at least 200 mm of rain a year. Rainfall is not harvests; it says nothing about prices, pests or irrigation since 2010. “Rain in El Niño years” is the average of the ten strongest El Niño years since 1981 against all years, a rough guide from ten cases.

    Far by vehicle

    an hour or more from the nearest health facility by motor vehicle, Malaria Atlas Project 2019. It assumes a vehicle is available and the road is open.

    Dark at night

    settled places (100 or more people per square kilometre) where the VIIRS satellite recorded almost no light at night in each of the last three years (average radiance under 0.5 nW/cm²/sr, 2023–2025). Mostly places without electricity, but villages with a few dim lights also read as dark, especially in steep terrain.

    Near fighting

    within 50 km of a place (a cell of about 10 km) where organised violence killed 25 or more people in 2021–2025, from the Uppsala Conflict Data Program’s georeferenced events (version 26.1), using events located to district level or better. The place card also gives the wider count, within 50 km of any deadly event. It covers wars, fighting between armed groups and attacks on civilians by them, not crime or violence at home, and it does not say that any one person was harmed.

    Not asked

    four questions about women’s lives, checked country by country: how many marry before 18, where women give birth, violence by a partner, and menstruation. A question counts as unanswered if UNICEF’s global database has no national figure since 2016 (child marriage, place of birth), if the WHO found no usable survey between 2000 and 2023 (partner violence), or if neither UNICEF nor the DHS programme holds any figure (menstruation). Only low- and middle-income countries are scored, because richer countries use other statistics. A national survey that these databases do not carry would be missed, so treat “not asked” as a lead to check. Province figures on the place card come from the latest DHS survey that reports by region.

    Unstudied

    for each province, the number of studies in Europe PMC (2010–2026) whose title or abstract names the province and mentions women, girls, gender, pregnancy or menstruation, and how many of those also mention a climate hazard such as flood, drought, heat, cold or cyclone. The headline counts provinces that at least five studies on women name, none of them with a climate hazard. Europe PMC indexes health and life-science research, so social science, reports and work in local languages are undercounted. Matching is by name: papers are not read. A province with a generic name such as “Central” is left out, and one that fewer than five studies name is marked “too few to tell”, because it may be unstudied or just spelled differently in the literature.

    Districts

    49,488 second-level units in 196 countries, from geoBoundaries v6.0.0, worked out the same way as provinces. Research and survey counts are not made for districts. Names are as the source spells them, so some are in the local script (Ukraine, Tunisia, Taiwan); 34 districts have no name in the source. Countries without a second level in the source have provinces only.

    Missing from the map

    about 125 very small islands and city districts are too small to draw at world scale. They are still counted in the totals and the list.

    Borders and names

    geoBoundaries v6.0.0. They are a data source, not a position on any disputed territory.

    What this is not

    a measure of harm, of what women experience, or of how well services work. It counts who lives where conditions are hard.

    Estimates of exposure, not measures of harm.
